If you've been using OpenRouter to access multiple AI models through a single API, you already know the appeal: one key, one endpoint, many models. But you've probably also hit the pain points, opaque pricing, credit expiry, and zero spend controls.

Ruvicode was built to fix exactly those problems. Let's break down how the two compare.

## Pricing transparency

OpenRouter charges a 5.5% markup on top of provider pricing, but you never see the exact per-request cost until after the fact. There's no `X-Cost` header, no per-request breakdown.

Ruvicode takes the opposite approach. Every response includes an `X-Cost` header showing exactly what you were charged, down to 8 decimal places. The pricing table on our website shows live $/1M token rates for every model, updated every 2 minutes.

## Cost savings

Ruvicode sources inference capacity through a competitive marketplace where sellers compete on price. The result: **20–77% cheaper** than OpenRouter across our model catalog.

For example, GLM-5.2 costs $0.95/1M on OpenRouter but only $0.22/1M on Ruvicode, a **77% savings**. GPT-5.4 drops from $2.50/1M to $1.00/1M, a 60% cut.

## Hard spend limits

This is OpenRouter's biggest weakness. There's no way to set a per-key spend cap. If your agent goes rogue and makes 10,000 requests overnight, you're on the hook for the full bill.

Ruvicode lets you set **daily, monthly, and total spend limits** on every API key. When a limit is hit, the key is automatically suspended. No surprise bills, ever.

## Credit expiry

OpenRouter expires your credits after a period of inactivity. Your prepaid balance literally disappears.

Ruvicode credits **never expire**. Your balance is yours, period.

## Payment flexibility

OpenRouter accepts cards and a few regional methods. If you're in a market where foreign cards are restricted, you're stuck.

Ruvicode accepts **global cards via Paddle** and **USDC cryptocurrency deposits** on the Base network. Borderless by default.

## The honest trade-off

Ruvicode has a smaller catalog (~20+ models vs OpenRouter's 400+). If you need exotic or niche models, OpenRouter has broader coverage. But for the 20+ frontier and popular models that most developers actually use, Ruvicode is cheaper, more transparent, and gives you real spend control.
